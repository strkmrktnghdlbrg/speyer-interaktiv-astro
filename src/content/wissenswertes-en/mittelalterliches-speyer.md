In the Middle Ages, Speyer was one of the most important cities of the Holy Roman Empire. A bishop's see, an imperial city, a trading place on the Rhine, the scene of imperial diets and the burial place of the [Salian emperors](/en/good-to-know/salian-emperors/): few cities in the German-speaking world combined as many roles as medieval Speyer.

## Craft guilds as the city's backbone

Economic life was organized in guilds. They regulated training, quality and prices, protected their members and took on social duties. Three particularly important guilds:

- **The gardeners** counted around 180 guild citizens in the 16th century and supplied the city with fruit and vegetables. The farming citizenry held a strong position in Speyer.
- **The clothmakers** long dominated the textile trade. Speyer cloth was known for its quality, though its importance declined in the late Middle Ages.
- **The smiths** produced tools, weapons, horseshoes and everyday goods. Their work was indispensable for the city and its surroundings.

The guilds were also politically important: they fought the patrician ruling class for seats on the council.

## Wine trade and economy

Speyer was one of the most important **wine trading cities on the Rhine**. The Palatinate produced Riesling, Pinot Noir and Dornfelder; the Rhine provided the transport route. Speyer merchants brokered wine to other regions of Germany and to the north. The wine economy shaped the city's inn culture, warehouse buildings and social life well beyond the Middle Ages.

## The market square as a trading center

Economic and social life played out on the market square. Food, textiles, tools and luxury goods changed hands here. Market days were social events as well, with festivities, performances and proclamations. Market revenues financed buildings, fortifications and municipal infrastructure.

## Buildings and cityscape

The cityscape was dominated by the [Imperial Cathedral](/en/attractions/imperial-cathedral/) as its towering center, along with other churches and chapels, the town hall and the city wall with its gates and towers - from the [Altpörtel gate](/en/attractions/altpoertel-gate/) to the Fishermen's Gate. Townhouses long stood in half-timber: a wooden frame filled with clay and straw. Only over time did stone walls and tiled roofs prevail, to reduce the danger of fire. The **city fire of 1689**, set by French troops in the War of the Palatine Succession, destroyed most of the medieval fabric. What we experience as the Old Town today was largely rebuilt after that fire.

## The life of the craftsmen

Living and working were closely intertwined: the workshop usually occupied the ground floor of the house, with sales directly on the street. Families worked together, children grew into the trade, and women often helped run workshops - as widows, they sometimes continued them independently. Women were organized in some guilds and could at times acquire independent rights, though they partly lost them again in the late Middle Ages.

## Imperial city and imperial diets

As a free imperial city, Speyer was directly subordinate to the emperor, which meant self-government and tax privileges. The city regularly hosted imperial diets, court assemblies and sessions of the Imperial Chamber Court (which had its seat here from 1527). These assemblies brought emperors, princes and diplomats to the city and made Speyer a political focal point of the Empire.

## Cultural traditions

The annual **historical city spectacle** symbolically transports Speyer back to the Middle Ages: knights' tournaments, jugglers, medieval music and craft stalls bring squares and lanes to life and show how deeply the medieval identity is rooted. ## Frequently asked questions

### Why was Speyer so important in the Middle Ages?

Speyer was a bishop's see, a free imperial city, the burial place of the Salian emperors and the venue of major imperial diets. The combination of these roles made the city a center of the Empire.

### Which guilds were influential in Speyer?

Gardeners, clothmakers and smiths were among the most important guilds. They structured the economy and city politics.

### What made the wine trade so important?

Speyer lay on the Rhine, the natural transport route for Palatine wine. For centuries the city was one of the Empire's most important wine trading hubs.

### Why doesn't the Old Town look medieval?

The fire of 1689 destroyed most of the medieval fabric. Today's Old Town is largely a Baroque reconstruction on the medieval street plan.

### What role did women play in the crafts?

Women worked in family businesses and could continue workshops as widows. Some guilds admitted women. With the economic crises at the end of the Middle Ages, their latitude shrank.
